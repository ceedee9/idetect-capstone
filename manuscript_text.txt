CHAPTER I THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND
Background of the Study
Senior citizens and persons with disabilities (PWDs) in the Philippines are legally entitled
to significant government-mandated benefits under Republic Acts 9994 (Expanded Senior
Citizens Act of 2010) and 10754 (An Act Expanding the Benefits and Privileges of Persons with
Disability). These landmark legislations guarantee a 20% discount on goods and services and
exemption from the 12% value-added tax (VAT), representing the government's commitment to
protecting vulnerable populations and ensuring equitable access to essential commodities and
services.
However, the implementation of these mandated discount programs faces a critical crisis
that undermines their fundamental purpose. The proliferation of fraudulent identification cards
has reached alarming proportions, with Senate investigations revealing that fake PWD IDs
outnumber legitimate credentials by nearly 5 to 1 approximately 8.5 million fraudulent IDs
circulating compared to only 1.89 million legitimately registered PWDs (Bordey, 2024). This
systematic exploitation resulted in ₱88.2 billion in lost government tax revenues in 2023 alone,
as calculated by the Bureau of Internal Revenue, which has classified fraudulent PWD ID use as
criminal tax evasion (Arceo, 2024).
The business sector bears substantial operational and financial burdens from this
widespread fraud. Restaurant operators documented dramatic increases in discount claims from
approximately 5% of transactions in 2019 to over 30% by 2024, with industry associations

Recognizing these challenges, local government units have initiated various anti-fraud
measures. Valenzuela City Government launched the "Bawal ang Fake ID" campaign and
introduced the VALID (Valenzuela ID Validation) Portal in May 2024, featuring newly designed
ID cards with holographic seals and web-based verification capabilities (City Government of
Valenzuela, 2024). Similarly, other municipalities developed dedicated platforms including the
OSCA Taguig Senior Citizen ID Verification System and PDAO Taguig PWD ID Checker. At
the national level, the Department of Social Welfare and Development announced development
of a unified PWD ID system targeted for implementation by 2025 (Serquiña, 2024), while
Bacolod City implemented QR-coded credentials to strengthen authentication (Bacolod City
Government, 2024).
Despite these initiatives, a critical gap persists in the verification ecosystem. Existing
systems require manual data entry consuming 30-45 seconds per transaction, operate as one-way
verification tools without mechanisms to automatically report suspicious IDs back to issuing
authorities, are geographically restricted to single municipalities preventing cross-jurisdictional
verification, and function as isolated solutions rather than integrated networks enabling
coordinated fraud intelligence. Contemporary research on digital identity verification
demonstrates that integrated systems achieve 70%+ fraud reduction through coordinated
intelligence sharing and real-time cross-verification (Protecht Group, 2025), yet no current
Philippine platform combines automated data capture, real-time dual-database verification,
mobile accessibility for point-of-sale environments, and bidirectional fraud reporting within a
unified architecture.

International examples provide proven architectural patterns for addressing these
challenges. Estonia's X-Road infrastructure processes over 2.7 billion data queries annually
through a decentralized data exchange layer connecting independent agency databases,
demonstrating that distributed verification architectures can maintain institutional sovereignty
while enabling comprehensive integration (Team, 2026). Research on optical character
recognition establishes that contemporary systems achieve 95-99% accuracy on standardized
documents, with mobile implementations capable of real-time processing at 62 frames per
second on mid-range smartphones (Liu et al., 2022; Gayer et al., 2023). These technological
capabilities, when adapted to Philippine implementation contexts, create opportunities for
transformative improvements in discount program verification.
Contemporary data privacy legislation establishes beneficiary rights extending beyond
passive credential verification. The Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act 10173, Section 16)
grants data subjects the right to reasonable access to their personal information and the ability to
dispute inaccuracies or errors in such data. This legal framework, combined with Department of
Justice clarifications requiring provisional approval even for unverifiable credentials, creates
regulatory expectations that verification systems must enable beneficiary participation in
validation processes rather than positioning vulnerable populations as passive subjects of
surveillance and control.

Project Context
This study is situated within the broader context of digital transformation efforts in
Philippine local governance, specifically addressing the intersection of social welfare service
delivery, fraud prevention, and technology-enabled business-government coordination. The
project responds to the documented systemic failures in Senior Citizen and PWD discount
verification detailed in the preceding section, where conventional manual approaches have
proven inadequate in preventing the exploitation crisis.
The Philippine implementation context presents unique challenges absent in international
digital identity systems. Unlike Estonia's unified infrastructure with 99% digital ID adoption or
Singapore's centralized SingPass platform, Philippine beneficiary data resides in over 1,780
independent local government unit databases with varying digitization levels, technical
sophistication, and data quality standards. Research by the University of the Philippines Center
for Local and Regional Governance found that only 60% of Philippine LGUs have established
operational Persons with Disability Affairs Offices as mandated by RA 10070, implying that
40% of municipalities and cities lack dedicated offices for PWD services, credential issuance,
and verification support (Yang, 2018).
This institutional fragmentation creates fundamental verification challenges. Senior
Citizen and PWD identification cards lack standardized formats across issuing authorities, with
designs varying by municipality (Respicio Law, 2024). While the National Council on Disability
Affairs announced a unified PWD ID design in January 2024, actual implementation across all

Problem Statement
The continued misuse of fake Senior Citizen and PWD identification cards has created a
multi-dimensional crisis affecting government revenues, business operations, and legitimate
beneficiaries' access to statutory entitlements. As documented in Section 1.1, the proliferation of
fraudulent credentials has reached unprecedented proportions, with 8.5 million fake PWD IDs
circulating compared to only 1.89 million legitimate registrations a 4.5:1 ratio that fundamentally
undermines the integrity of government-mandated discount programs under Republic Acts 9994
and 10754.
The economic consequences are severe and measurable. Government tax revenues lost
₱88.2 billion in 2023 due to fraudulent discount transactions, while businesses absorbed ₱166.1
billion in fraudulent discounts exceeding the ₱42.3 billion provided to legitimate beneficiaries
(Bordey, 2024). One major restaurant chain's experience exemplifies operational impact,
rejecting 17.24% of presented PWD IDs as suspected fraudulent over a three-month period,
representing nearly one in five credentials failing basic authenticity checks (Arceo, 2024). These
verification failures translate to substantial financial losses for establishments: the combined

General Objective
To design, develop, and implement iDetect, an integrated OCR-based identification
verification system that will connect food service establishments in Valenzuela City with
government databases (OSCA and PDAO) through a multi-portal architecture enabling efficient,
accurate, and automated validation of Senior Citizen and PWD identification cards. The system
will provide establishments with a reliable, secure, and mobile-accessible platform for verifying
credentials, preventing fraud through coordinated intelligence sharing, and facilitating real-time
bidirectional communication with local government units to ensure the integrity of
government-mandated discount programs under Republic Acts 9994 and 10754.
The system will operate through five integrated portals serving distinct stakeholder
groups: (1) a Client Portal (mobile application) enabling business establishments to perform
automated OCR-based verification with real-time database cross-checking, (2) a BPLO Portal
(web-based) enabling the Business Permit and Licensing Office to manage establishment
accounts and monitor technical operations without accessing sensitive beneficiary data, (3) an
OSCA Portal (web-based) enabling the Office of Senior Citizens Affairs to receive flagged
Senior Citizen IDs, investigate suspicious records, and update official databases, and (4) a
PDAO Portal (web-based) providing parallel functionality for Persons with Disability Affairs
Office to manage PWD credential verification and fraud investigation, and (5) a Beneficiary
Mobile Application enabling Senior Citizens and PWDs to verify their ID status in real-time,
receive transaction notifications, dispute erroneous denials through structured review processes
managed by OSCA/PDAO staff, and report unauthorized use of their credentials, Through this

government units. As established in the problem statement, the project addresses the documented
crisis where fake IDs outnumber legitimate credentials 4.5 to 1.

Routine Activity Theory (Cohen & Felson, 1979)
Routine Activity Theory (RAT), originally developed by Cohen and Felson (1979) in
criminology, posits that crime occurs when three elements converge in time and space: (1) a
motivated offender willing to commit fraud, (2) a suitable target that can be exploited, and (3)
the absence of a capable guardian providing effective oversight or protection. According to RAT,
crime prevention is most effective when interventions focus on reducing target suitability and
increasing capable guardianship rather than solely addressing offender motivation, as altering
criminal inclinations is substantially more difficult than modifying situational opportunities for
crime commission.

Fraud Management Lifecycle (Wilhelm, 2004)
The Fraud Management Lifecycle (FML) Theory, developed by Wilhelm (2004),
provides a holistic and systematic approach to fraud management through eight interconnected
stages that form a dynamic, evolving, and adaptive cycle. This framework recognizes that
effective fraud management extends beyond simple detection to encompass comprehensive

lifecycle management from deterrence through prosecution, with each stage playing a critical
role in overall fraud prevention and mitigation.
Wilhelm (2004) emphasizes that "effective fraud management requires a balance in the
competing and complementary actions within the Fraud Management Lifecycle, highlighting that
success depends not on focusing exclusively on any single stage but rather on coordinated
activity across all stages. The framework has been validated across five industries with
significant economic crime, demonstrating functional applicability across various organizational
contexts including banking, telecommunications, insurance, and retail sectors (Wilhelm, 2004).
The Eight Stages of the Fraud Management Lifecycle:
1. Deterrence: The first stage is characterized by actions and activities intended to stop or
prevent fraud before it is attempted; that is, to turn aside or discourage even the attempt at fraud
(Wilhelm, 2004). Deterrence mechanisms create psychological and practical barriers that
dissuade potential fraudsters from attempting fraudulent activities. In the context of identity
verification systems, the visible presence of technology-enabled authentication serves as a
deterrent signal that fraudulent credentials will be detected.
2. Prevention: Prevention involves proactive actions and activities designed to prevent fraud
from occurring by eliminating opportunities for fraudulent exploitation (Wilhelm, 2004). Unlike
deterrence which focuses on discouraging attempts, prevention focuses on blocking execution
through system controls, validation mechanisms, and access restrictions that make fraud
technically difficult or impossible to complete.

3. Detection: Detection stage involves actions and activities, such as statistical monitoring
programs, used to identify and locate fraud prior to, during, and subsequent to the completion of
the fraudulent activity (Wilhelm, 2004). The intent of detection is to uncover or reveal the
presence of fraud or fraud attempts through automated systems, pattern recognition, anomaly
detection, and manual review processes that identify discrepancies between presented credentials
and authoritative records.
4. Mitigation: The goal of mitigation is to stop losses from occurring or continuing to occur
and/or to hinder a fraudster from continuing or completing the fraudulent activity (Wilhelm,
2004). Wilhelm emphasizes that successful fraud management revolves around the appropriate
allocation of sufficient, efficient, and early mitigation efforts, as timely intervention directly
reduces financial impact and prevents fraud propagation.
5. Analysis: Analysis involves systematic study of fraud incidents using root cause analysis,
pattern recognition, and data analytics to understand fraud factors, identify trends, and extract
actionable intelligence from fraud events (Wilhelm, 2004). This stage transforms individual
fraud incidents into organizational learning, enabling continuous improvement of fraud
management capabilities through evidence-based insights.
6. Policy: The policy stage encompasses creation, evaluation, and deployment of fraud reduction
policies based on analysis findings and organizational requirements (Wilhelm, 2004). Effective
policies translate fraud intelligence into actionable guidelines, controls, and procedures that

extraction of text from images, converting printed or handwritten characters into
machine-readable digital format. In identity verification contexts, OCR serves as the critical first
step by extracting data from identification documents such as IDs, and government-issued
credentials. Modern OCR systems have evolved from template-matching approaches to deep
learning-based implementations capable of processing complex document layouts with high
accuracy rates.

recognition accuracy. This high accuracy makes OCR viable for automated verification systems
where manual data entry would be time-prohibitive. However, accuracy varies significantly with
image quality. Research comparing OCR engines on typewritten documents found that

Philippine Senior Citizen and PWD Discount Implementation Challenges
Philippine discount program implementation faces systemic challenges rooted in
institutional capacity limitations, infrastructure fragmentation, and verification system
inadequacies. Republic Act 9994 (Expanded Senior Citizens Act of 2010) and Republic Act
10754 (Expanding Benefits for Persons with Disability) mandate 20% discounts plus VAT
exemptions, with implementing rules directing provisions be 'construed liberally and in favor' of
beneficiaries when interpretation conflicts arise. However, the Department of Justice opinion
issued January 2024 clarified that establishments cannot legally withhold privileges even when
unable to verify authenticity immediately, noting the Department of Health PWD Registry 'does
not yet contain complete listing' for various reasons (Cervantes, 2024). This places
establishments in untenable positions legally required to honor potentially fraudulent credentials
or face ₱50,000-100,000 fines and 2-6 years imprisonment, yet lacking verification mechanisms
distinguishing legitimate from fraudulent claims.
Research by the University of the Philippines Center for Local and Regional Governance
found that only 60% of Philippine LGUs established operational Persons with Disability Affairs
Offices as mandated by RA 10070, with 33 provinces, 25 cities, and 282 municipalities lacking
dedicated PDAO offices (Alampay et al., 2018). This implies 40% of municipalities and cities
hundreds of LGUs across the archipelago lack dedicated offices for PWD services, credential
issuance, and verification support. The advocacy report noted PDAO absence 'impaired
implementation of Republic Act 10754,' demonstrating direct linkage between institutional
capacity and program effectiveness. Funding structure compounds disparities as RA 9994
specifies that 'necessary appropriations for operation and maintenance shall be appropriated and

Identification standardization challenges compound capacity gaps. Unlike national ID

Key Findings from Literature Review
The literature review reveals five fundamental findings informing iDetect's design:
OCR Technology and Accuracy: The foundational viability of the iDetect system is
supported by the high-performance standards of modern Optical Character Recognition (OCR).
Research by Liu et al. (2022) establishes a benchmark of 99.25% character recognition accuracy,
suggesting that automated extraction is a reliable alternative to manual entry. Industry research
further demonstrates that OCR-based systems achieve accuracy rates of 99.95-99.99% compared
to manual entry accuracy of 96-99%, with manual transcription error rates reaching 3.7% in
laboratory settings, of which 14.2% contain potentially dangerous discrepancies (Martinez, 2025;
Mays et al., 2019). However, the transition from laboratory settings to point-of-sale
environments introduces significant variables. Hamdi et al. (2019) and Stančić & Trbušić (2020)
observe that environmental factors such as blurred or skewed images can cause accuracy to
degrade as low as 50% error rate. Furthermore, Lerouge et al. (2024) posits that field-level
accuracy, specifically for critical data like ID numbers, is more difficult to maintain than
individual character recognition. These findings necessitate iDetect's focus on robust

Project Description
Project Design
This study employs an Applied and Descriptive Research Design to develop, implement,
and evaluate the iDetect system, an OCR-based ID verification application for Business
Establishments under the Business Permit and Licensing Office in Valenzuela City.
Applied research is a type of scientific inquiry directed toward solving specific, practical
problems by translating theoretical knowledge into actionable solutions within a real-world
context, characterized by its intent to produce findings that are immediately useful to
practitioners, policymakers, and institutions facing concrete operational challenges (Creswell,
2014). Descriptive research, on the other hand, systematically describes and documents
phenomena, conditions, or characteristics as they currently exist without manipulating variables,
thereby providing an accurate and comprehensive account of the subject under investigation
(Kothari, 2004). The convergence of these two approaches enables the research to
simultaneously capture the existing landscape of ID verification practices and develop a
concrete, evidence-based solution to the identified problems.
The applied dimension of this study is grounded in the documented inadequacies of
current manual ID verification practices in Valenzuela City, including ₱88.2 billion in lost
government revenues attributed to fraudulent ID use. In response, iDetect implements automated
cross-verification against authoritative databases, eliminating reliance on subjective visual

routing to investigation authorities and real-time synchronization across all system users.
Through OCR automation, the mobile-first design targets a reduction in verification time to 5-8

The descriptive dimension of the study systematically documents the current state of

Research Locale
This study will be conducted in Valenzuela City, a highly urbanized city in the National
Capital Region (NCR) of the Philippines. Valenzuela City serves as an ideal research locale due
to its active implementation of anti-fraud measures through the "Bawal ang Fake ID" campaign
and the VALID (Valenzuela ID Validation) Portal launched in May 2024, demonstrating local
government commitment to addressing fraudulent identification issues.
The research will focus specifically on food service establishments throughout
Valenzuela City, including restaurants, fast-food chains, cafes, and dining establishments that
regularly process Senior Citizen and PWD discount transactions under Republic Acts 9994 and
10754. These establishments were selected as the primary implementation sites because food
service businesses represent the sector most significantly impacted by fraudulent discount
claims, with industry data showing discount claims increasing from approximately 5% of
transactions in 2019 to over 30% by 2024.

the research objectives. This approach is appropriate for developmental and applied research as it
ensures participation of information-rich respondents who possess direct experience with the
verification challenges the iDetect system aims to address.
